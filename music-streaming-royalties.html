Understanding Music Streaming Royalties

Learn the basic concepts behind streaming revenue, music ownership, distributors and royalty reporting.

5 lessons30 minutes

What you will learn

  • Understand basic music rights.
  • Learn the role of music distributors.
  • Understand why streaming payments vary.
  • Read royalty statements carefully.
  • Keep accurate music records.

Course lessons

Lesson 1: Understand the different music rights

A recording and the underlying composition are different assets. A sound recording may have a master owner, while the composition involves songwriters and publishers. Different rights can generate different types of royalties.

Task: Write down the performers, songwriters and recording owners involved in one of your songs.

Lesson 2: Learn what a distributor does

A digital distributor can deliver recordings and metadata to music services and collect certain payments on behalf of the rights holder. Services, fees, territories and payment terms differ, so read the agreement before signing up.

Task: Identify the distributor and account responsible for one release.

Lesson 3: Why streaming revenue varies

There is no single guaranteed payment for every stream. Revenue can depend on the service, listener location, subscription type, advertising, rights arrangements and the distributor's terms. Public estimates should not be treated as guaranteed income.

Task: List three reasons two artists might receive different amounts.

Lesson 4: Read royalty statements

Check the reporting period, track identifiers, territories, usage figures, deductions, currency and payment thresholds. Keep statements so you can compare reports and ask informed questions if something looks incorrect.

Task: Make a spreadsheet to record release, reporting period, gross amount, deductions and net payment.

Lesson 5: Keep your music records organized

Store agreements, split sheets, credits, registration details, distributor statements and payment records securely. Make sure collaborators agree on ownership and splits in writing before a release wherever possible.

Task: Create a folder for one release and organize its rights and payment documents.
